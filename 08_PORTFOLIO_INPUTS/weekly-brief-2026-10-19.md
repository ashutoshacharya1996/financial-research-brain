## What the Engine Found This Week

### Research Queue — Only 6 Stocks, Not 10

These are not buy calls. Think of this as a shortlist of restaurants to read reviews for. This month the reviews are mixed, and only six restaurants are even on the map. Slots 7–10 stay empty instead of being padded with weak names.

The order below is "least messy first". It is not "most exciting first".

**1. Larsen & Toubro (LT)**
*Why it made the list:* It is the only company whose pile of signed-but-undelivered work is still growing (up 27% from a year ago).
*What this means:* Think of a builder with a full diary of jobs. The diary is great, but the builder is finishing jobs slower, keeping less profit per job, and cash went out faster than it came in last quarter. The diary is real. Whether it turns into profit is the open question.

**2. Bharat Electronics (BEL)**
*Why it made the list:* Sales grew 25% from a year ago, and customers are paying faster (about 140 days, down from 176).
*What this means:* Sales are up but the pile of future work shrank slightly (down 2.2%), and the big missile contract everyone was waiting for (QRSAM) has still not been found signed. Also, the bosses gave two different forecasts on the same call. More on that below.

**3. Paras Defence & Space (PARAS)**
*Why it made the list:* Its main product line (optics for defence) grew 62% from a year ago, and its credit rating was upgraded.
*What this means:* The core business looks healthy. But shareholders voted down a deal with related companies, and the company announced a Rs 6,200 crore chip-factory plan that is about 13 times last year's sales, with no word on who pays for it.

**4. JNK India (JNKINDIA)**
*Why it made the list:* The list of possible future deals grew to over Rs 6,000 crore, and its credit outlook moved to Stable.
*What this means:* Possible deals are not signed deals. Its signed-work pile fell 8.2%, a big UAE order was cancelled in July, and it has two different sets of FY27 targets on record.

**5. Gabriel India (GABRIEL)**
*Why it made the list:* Demand for its shock absorbers is fine, and its credit rating went up.
*What this means:* Profit per rupee of sales slipped, and the company is now spending big on buying a business linked to its own promoters. The auditor did not review the restated comparison numbers.

**6. Hindustan Aeronautics (HAL)**
*Why it made the list:* Sales and profit both grew about 14–15%.
*What this means:* It ranks last because the company stopped telling us its order book, margins and targets. It is like a student who hands in the answer but not the working. There is also a board committee that could not be reconstituted, and a related-party takeover that the filing itself says was not at arm's length.

**7–10. Not filled.** Only six companies are tracked. Nothing from outside the list was added.

### The Big Ideas This Week — Both Are Weaker Than in June

Honest note: in June the engine showed three big ideas. Today only two still count, and neither deserves the "high conviction" label. Both have been moved from Active to Monitoring. The third June idea has dropped out entirely (see below).

---

**Idea #1: India's Defence Electronics Boom — Cooled Down**

*What's happening, in plain English:*
Imagine a shop where everyone swore a huge delivery truck would arrive by September. It is now late October and nobody has seen the truck. That truck is QRSAM, a large air-defence missile contract (about Rs 21,000 crore for BEL by June's files). BEL's boss said "maximum by September" on 27 July. The date has passed and no signing shows up in the files we have.

*Why now specifically:*
- QRSAM is still the big swing factor. The engine's rule is that one more slip turns this theme from "Weakening" to "Declining". Note: not finding the signing in our files is not proof it has not happened.
- BEL said two other big orders could land in "3 to 6 months" from late July: Shatrughat/Samaghat (about Rs 9,000 crore) and HAMMER (about Rs 2,500 crore, "this financial year"). That window is opening about now.
- HAL's next results and the completion of its HATSOFF takeover (about early December) are the first chances to get its order book and margin numbers back.

*The companies to look at:*
BEL, HAL, PARAS.
BEL has the cleanest sales growth. PARAS has the best operating number (optics). HAL has stopped giving the numbers we need.

*What could go wrong:*
- Three of the four companies in this theme have a governance problem: BEL's conflicting forecasts, HAL's board committee and related-party takeover, PARAS's defeated shareholder vote.
- Part of the drop in score is lost visibility. HAL and PARAS stopped reporting key numbers. That is not the same as proven bad news, but it is a reason the engine cannot confirm good news either.

*How confident is the data:* 5.5/10, down from 9.0 in June. Half-confident at best. The theme score fell from 9.2 to 6.8, and the trend is Weakening. In June the data pointed one way. Now it is mixed, with the key June catalyst (QRSAM) unconfirmed.

*Your next move:* Check the NSE announcements for BEL for any QRSAM or Shatrughat/Samaghat "Bagging / Receiving of Orders" filing. This is the single fact that most changes the picture.

**Idea #2: India's Factory-Building Boom — Still Uneven, Now Weaker**

*What's happening, in plain English:*
Think of a town where everyone said they would renovate their houses. Some have started, but the builders are short of materials and some owners changed their minds. The money is being talked about more than it is being spent. Of the six companies, only LT's pile of future work is still growing. BEL and JNK India's piles shrank, and HAL does not say.

*Why now specifically:*
- LT's Q2 results (late October / November) are the first test of whether the second half of the year picks up, as management hopes.
- Two LT milestones have dates: the Data Center transfer (due by 31 October) and the Hyderabad Metro closing (31 December).
- JNK India says refinery and fertilizer deals should be finalised in Q2–Q3. So far only two small flare orders (Rs 0–100 crore each) have been filed.

*The companies to look at:*
LT, JNKINDIA, BEL.
LT has the only growing backlog. JNK has a big pipeline but a shrinking signed book. BEL is in both themes.

*What could go wrong:*
- Five of the six companies carry the governance flag. Until BEL and JNK clarify their forecasts, the numbers cannot be used for estimates.
- LT earns a lot of its new work (56%) in the Middle East, and also blames the Middle East for slow delivery. Same region, both the good news and the bad news.

*How confident is the data:* 5.0/10, down from 7.5 in June. Weak. Six companies are named, but only one or two have clean good evidence.

*Your next move:* Open LT's Q2 FY27 results or call on 27 October. Look for three numbers: cash from operations (was negative Rs 20.2 billion in Q1), revenue growth (needs 10% or more), and EBITDA margin (needs to be back near 9.9%).

## The Open Questions

These are the things the engine could not settle. Each one limits how much weight any of the above can bear.

1. **BEL gave two forecasts on the same call.** The same boss said FY27 profit margin would be 28% and also 21–23%. He said new orders would be Rs 55,000 crore and also "more than Rs 20,000 crore". The 28% / Rs 55,000 crore set is repeated more often and matches earlier statements, so it looks more likely correct. That is a guess, not an answer. If the lower set is right, the order forecast drops by about two-thirds. The recording is not in our files, so a human needs to listen to it or the company needs to clarify in writing.
2. **QRSAM is unsigned in our files.** Maybe it is not signed. Maybe it is signed and we missed it. We cannot tell.
3. **Call transcripts are missing** for LT, GABRIEL and PARAS for Q1 FY27. Without them, nobody can check what management said about their targets.
4. **Kite was not logged in.** There is no current share price and no view of what you actually hold. So price versus fair value is "Unknown", not "fine". There is also no slot assigned to any of these names in your portfolio.
5. **Some of the score drop is lost visibility, not bad news.** HAL and PARAS stopped giving order book, margin and targets.

## What Changed vs June (17 Weeks Ago)

- **Conviction fell across the board.** Defence went from 9.0 to 5.5. Capex went from 7.5 to 5.0. Both moved from Active to Monitoring. The June reasons (QRSAM "imminent", PARAS's first-ever growth guidance, JNK's UAE order, Gabriel's sunroof plan) were not confirmed or were reversed.
- **The ranking reshuffled.** June order: PARAS, BEL, GABRIEL, HAL, JNKINDIA, LT. Now: LT, BEL, PARAS, JNKINDIA, GABRIEL, HAL. LT jumped from 6th to 1st mostly because the others fell, not because LT got much better.
- **A new warning layer was added.** It looks for companies whose forecasts contradict themselves or whose deals benefit insiders. It scored 7.0, and it affects five of the six names.
- **PARAS's June growth promise (30–40%) was not repeated.** The results land inside that range, but the company never restated it.
- **Gabriel's June good news reversed.** Profit margin fell from 9.8% to 8.7% a year on, and its capital is now going into a promoter-linked purchase.

## Jargon Glossary

| Term used | What it actually means |
|---|---|
| Q1 FY27 | The April–June 2026 quarter, the first three months of the financial year ending March 2027 |
| Quarterly refresh | A catch-up run that processes a whole quarter of filings at once instead of one week's |
| Order book / signed-work pile | The total value of contracts a company has won but not yet delivered |
| Order inflow | New contracts signed in a quarter |
| Pipeline | Deals the company hopes to win. Not signed yet |
| Book-to-bill | Rupees of new orders signed for every rupee of work delivered. Below 1.0 means the pile is shrinking |
| EBITDA margin | How much profit the company keeps from each rupee of sales after running costs, before tax |
| PAT | Profit After Tax, the final profit number |
| Management guidance / forecast | What the company's bosses said publicly about what to expect next |
| Conviction / confidence score | How strongly the evidence supports a theme. 5.5/10 means about half-convinced |
| Weakening trend | The evidence is getting less convincing over time |
| Monitoring | Watched but not treated as active. Below the 6.0 level needed for the watchlist |
| Governance overlay | A warning layer for contradictory forecasts, unfair related-party deals and similar red flags |
| Related-party deal | A deal between the company and another business linked to its owners or insiders |
| Keep Tracking | Keep collecting evidence, nothing more. It is not a view on the share |
| QRSAM | A large air-defence missile system contract that BEL is waiting to have signed |
| Receivable days | How many days customers take to pay. Lower is better |
| Operating cash flow | Cash actually coming in from running the business. Negative means more cash went out than came in |
| NSE filing | A required public announcement from a listed company on the National Stock Exchange |
| Kite | The broker account connection used to pull live prices and holdings |
| Slot | A planned place for a stock in your portfolio. None is assigned yet |
