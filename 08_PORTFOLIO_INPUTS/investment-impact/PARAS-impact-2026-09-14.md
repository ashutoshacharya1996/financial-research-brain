## Opportunity Signal

**What changed this week:** At PARAS's 17th AGM (Sept 11, 2026), shareholders rejected Resolution No. 6 — a Material Related-Party Transaction with subsidiary Paras Anti-Drone Technologies Private Limited — with 56.80% of votes cast against it (3,175,312 against vs. 2,414,946 in favour). This is the first shareholder-vote failure in PARAS's tracked history. A second RPT item on the same ballot (Resolution 5, with associate company Controp-Paras Technologies Pvt Ltd) passed, but with only 58.57% in favour (41.43% against) — an unusually high opposition rate for a passed item.

**Why it matters:** The rejected transaction touches PARAS's own anti-drone subsidiary, which is embedded in the company's forward growth narrative (alongside its core electro-optics/imaging business). Shareholders exercising real voting discipline against a related-party item — rather than rubber-stamping it — is a governance inflection for a company that, until June 2026, had no earnings calls and had just begun building institutional visibility via a London roadshow with GIC and Temasek. It does not directly contradict the order-book/growth thesis, but it introduces an unresolved trust question layered directly on top of it, at a company that is PARAS's smallest-cap, least-liquid, and least-disclosed name in its peer cluster.

## Fact Check

**What we assume this signal implies:** A rejected related-party transaction with a company's own subsidiary usually signals either (a) shareholders spotted a specific, material problem with the transaction's terms or governance process, or (b) a broader skepticism toward the company's related-party dealings generally (supported here by Resolution 5 also drawing 41.4% opposition despite passing).

**What we verified:**
- Resolution 6 rejection confirmed via primary-source NSE Reg. 44(3) filing: "Resolution no. 6 was not passed with requisite majority" — exact vote counts and percentages stated (56.80% against).
- Resolution 5's narrow passage (58.57% in favour) is confirmed in the same filing — both RPT items on the ballot drew unusually high opposition, indicating a pattern rather than an isolated failure.
- Procedural detail confirmed: Chairman Mr. Sharad Virji Shah did not attend the AGM (MD Mr. Munjal Sharad Shah chaired); for Item 6 specifically, an Independent Director (Dr. Hina Gokhale) had to chair because the MD was "concerned or interested" in that item — standard recusal procedure, but notable given the item's failure.
- FY27/FY28 revenue guidance (30–40%, first issued June 2026) was referenced only as a general "overview... and future outlook" at the AGM — not verbatim reiterated, not revised. This is a gap, not a withdrawal, and is explicitly flagged as such in the delta.
- This signal is explicitly stated in the source filing (vote percentages, resolution text), not inferred — it meets the fact-check bar. Freshness: 3 days old at analysis date, well within the 30-day threshold.

**What we still don't know:**
- The specific reasons shareholders voted against Resolution 6 — no filing, press statement, or proxy-advisory note discloses this
- Whether the rejected transaction's terms will be renegotiated and re-tabled, abandoned, or restructured
- Whether the FY27/FY28 30–40% guidance still stands — it was referenced but not verbatim-confirmed at the AGM
- FY26 dividend amount (agenda item 2 approved in principle; amount not disclosed in the proceedings summary)
