## Game Theory View

Who captures the economics of the pattern? Controlling shareholders and promoter groups gain flexibility (GABRIEL consolidating Anand Group automotive assets; HAL as sole shareholder on its JVs); minority holders carry the valuation risk but have a veto tool in the AGM vote (PARAS Resolution 6 shows it works when public institutions are organised).
Who gets squeezed? Public minority shareholders in companies where the promoter is a counterparty to large transactions.
Who gains bargaining power? Rating agencies and auditors (Crisil's integration-risk language on GABRIEL; PwC's scope limitation) and institutional holders who can now cite a precedent for voting down RPTs.

## Counter Arguments

- Most items are disclosed, valued by independent valuers and shareholder-approved (GABRIEL: 2026-08-19 AGM; Crisil upgraded to AA+/Stable); disclosure is evidence of process, not of misconduct.
