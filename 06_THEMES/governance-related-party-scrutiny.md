## Potential Losers

- **PARAS** — the rejected RPT touches the company's own anti-drone subsidiary, part of its forward growth narrative; unresolved governance friction could delay or reshape that subsidiary's integration into the parent's growth story
- **GABRIEL** — minority shareholders face undisclosed dilution from the AIPL allotment; if the market prices in governance risk, GABRIEL's premium valuation (35x FY28 EPS per Motilal Oswal) has less room for error
- Any tracked company where a promoter/related-party transaction is pending shareholder approval — this week's evidence shows voting discipline is real, not rubber-stamp, which raises approval-process risk for future related-party items across the universe

## Game Theory View

**Who captures the economics:** Promoter/related-party groups attempting internal restructuring (GABRIEL's AIPL/Anand Group, PARAS's own subsidiary structure) captured the initial move — both transactions were structured and, in GABRIEL's case, executed before this week's scrutiny surfaced.

**Who gets squeezed:** Minority shareholders bear the dilution/governance risk without a disclosed valuation basis (GABRIEL) or see their subsidiary-level growth narrative complicated by an unresolved RPT rejection (PARAS).

**Who gains bargaining power:** Institutional/minority shareholders who vote against related-party items — PARAS's 56.8% rejection is direct evidence that shareholder voting blocs can stop a promoter-adjacent transaction, which raises the bargaining leverage of that voting bloc for future RPT items across the tracked universe, and increases the disclosure burden on companies proposing them.

## Counter Arguments

- Only 2 companies — below the corroboration depth needed for a structurally confirmed theme; needs a third company with a comparable adverse governance/RPT signal before this graduates past Emerging
- The two signals are mechanistically different (a lost shareholder vote vs. a board-approved allotment that shareholders had already pre-approved at the August 19, 2026 AGM) — GABRIEL's allotment was NOT rejected by shareholders, it was approved in principle in August and merely executed in September, which is a materially weaker adverse signal than PARAS's outright vote failure
- HAL's and BEL's auditor changes, while superficially "governance events" in the same week, are explicitly routine and cleared — including them would overstate corroboration breadth; excluding them is the more defensible reading of the evidence
- Neither company's underlying operating thesis (PARAS's defence electronics growth; GABRIEL's EV/auto-component pivot) has been contradicted by these governance events — this is a capital-structure/trust overlay, not evidence the core businesses are deteriorating
- Reasons for PARAS's RPT rejection are not disclosed in any filing this week — without knowing shareholders' specific objection, it is not yet possible to assess whether this is a one-off procedural issue or a genuine red flag
