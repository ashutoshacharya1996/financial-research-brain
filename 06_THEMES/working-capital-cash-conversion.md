## Game Theory View

**Who captures the economics:** Companies with large customer advances and milestone-billing power, such as LT on ultra-mega international EPC and BEL as a sole-source PSU to MoD, can make customers fund working capital.
**Who gets squeezed:** Tier-2 suppliers and auto ancillaries (GABRIEL, JNKINDIA), who carry receivables for OEMs and EPC primes. GABRIEL's receivables grew 25.8% in six months.
**Who gains bargaining power:** Buyers with payment discretion. LT's rising ECL on delayed I&U collections shows that state and utility clients can still push the cost back onto contractors.

## Counter Arguments

- **Timing, not structure** — BEL's OCF swing coincides with a ₹1,136cr fall in other current liabilities (advances consumed); LT's NWC benefits from advances on fresh ultra-mega orders. Both can reverse as execution catches up.
- **Receivable stress at LT** — ECL/bad debts ₹2,353cr in H1 (+26%) contradicts a clean working-capital improvement story.
- **Split universe** — two of six tracked companies (GABRIEL, JNKINDIA) deteriorated and HAL shows no improvement, so this is not a market-wide theme.
- **No duration** — first run with this signal; needs confirmation in Q3 FY27.
- **PARAS inventory build** (+21%) partly offsets the OCF improvement.
